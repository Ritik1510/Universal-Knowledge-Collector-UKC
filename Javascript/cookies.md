## Cookies
Cookies are small text files that websites place on your device. They act like a digital memory so the website can remember who you are.
## What Cookies Do

* Keep you logged in: They stop you from typing your password on every page.
* Save your settings: They remember your chosen language, dark mode theme, or location.
* Hold shopping carts: They store items in your cart while you browse other pages.
* Track site performance: They tell owners which pages are popular and where errors happen.

## How Cookies Work

   1. You visit a site: Your browser asks the website to load a page.
   2. The site sends a cookie: The website replies with the page data and a tiny text file.
   3. Your browser saves it: The text file is stored safely on your computer or phone.
   4. The site recognizes you: When you click another link, your browser sends that file back to the site.


## Why are first-party cookies and third-party cookies treated so differently?

First-party and third-party cookies are treated differently because of who controls them and how far their tracking reaches. First-party cookies stay on a single website to help you log in or save items in a cart. Third-party cookies follow you across different websites to build a profile for ads. [1, 2, 3, 4]  
Ownership and Scope 

- First-party cookies: Made by the exact site you visit. Only that site can read them. They stop working when you leave. 
- Third-party cookies: Made by an outside group, like an ad network. They load on many different sites. They share your data with outside companies. [1, 4, 5, 6]  

Purpose and Value 

- First-party cookies: Used to make a site work well. They remember your language, items in a shop cart, or login state. Users want them for a smooth visit. 
- Third-party cookies: Used for cross-site tracking. They watch what you do on site A, B, and C. They show you targeted ads based on your habits. [1, 3]  

Privacy and Rules 

- First-party cookies: Seen as safe and helpful. Laws like GDPR allow them if they are needed for the site to run. 
- Third-party cookies: Seen as a risk to personal privacy. Web browsers block them by default now. Regulators look closely at how they collect data without clear consent. [1, 2, 3, 9, 10]
